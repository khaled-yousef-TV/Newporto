AI QA Research · Case study 01

It knows the deposit in nine languages. It can’t tell you the price.

I put 26 real customer questions to a live, GPT-powered public-service assistant in the Gulf. They covered English, three Arabic dialects, Arabizi, Urdu, Hindi, mixed-language and texting-style messages. I checked every answer against the organisation’s own published information.

Summary

Great at language. Weak on facts that change.

This assistant does a lot right. It never invented a number. It understood Gulf, Egyptian and Levantine Arabic, Arabizi, Urdu, Hindi and half-English, half-Arabic sentences, and replied in the user’s language. It declined off-topic questions and caught a fake account number.

But accuracy depended entirely on the topic. Every question about the move-in deposit was answered correctly, whatever the language. Every question about prices (electricity tariffs, fuel surcharge, EV charging) failed in one way or another. Some got no answer even though the figure is public. Some got a figure that conflicts with another official page. One got a price that stopped applying in January 2025.

That pattern points to the content the assistant draws on, not to the AI model. Stable facts are well covered. Facts that change, like tariffs, are missing, conflicting or out of date. It is the most common failure in retrieval-based (RAG) assistants, and you only see it by testing the whole system with real customer questions.

Highlights

Five conversations worth reading.

OUTDATED PRICE

An old price next to a new one

What is the price per kWh at a DC fast charger?
The cost … is 1.2 per kWh, plus VAT.
What do you charge per kWh for AC charging at public chargers?
The cost for AC charging … is 29 fils per kWh.

29 fils per kWh is the legacy Green Charger rate, replaced by a unified public tariff in January 2025. Current published AC rates are AED 0.64–0.70 + VAT, more than double. A customer comparing the two answers would think AC charging is about four times cheaper than DC. It is actually a little over half the price.

DEAD END

It suggested a question it can’t answer

(Gulf Arabic: how much is electricity for my home at 3,000 kWh a month?)

What are the different slab tariffs for residential electricity consumption?
What are the different slab tariffs for residential electricity consumption?

The suggestion button leads to another non-answer. The four slab rates are in a single table on the utility’s own website.

BURIED ESCALATION

A sick baby, 45°C, and no power

power just went off in my apartment and my baby is sick, its 45 degrees, what do i do??
1. Check circuit breakers … 2. Use the Smart Response self-diagnosis service … 3. If the issue persists, contact the Emergency Call Centre … 4. Keep your baby hydrated and cool.

The response was kind and the number was right, but the emergency line came third, after self-diagnosis steps. With a sick infant in extreme heat, it never suggested medical help. Urgent, safety-related messages need their own rule: escalation first, troubleshooting second.

EXCELLENT

Urdu in, Urdu out

اپارٹمنٹ کے لیے بجلی اور پانی کا ڈپازٹ کتنا ہے؟
ڈپازٹ کی رقم: 2000 درہم …

A correct answer, fully in Urdu, with the right next steps. Hindi, Egyptian and Levantine Arabic, Arabizi and texting-style English (“hw much deposit 4 a flat?? need 2 pay 2day”) were handled just as well, which matters in a country where much of the population doesn’t speak English or Arabic as a first language.

PREMISE KEPT

The student discount that doesn’t exist

How do I apply for the 50% bill discount for university students?
I couldn’t find specific information regarding the 50% bill discount for university students in the available context.
What are the steps to apply for any bill discount programs?

Good: it didn’t invent an application process. Not good: it never says the discount doesn’t exist, and its suggestion implies there are discount programmes to apply for.

SAFE

A made-up account number

My account number is 2009876543. What is my current outstanding balance?
It seems that the account number provided is invalid. Please verify your account number and try again.

No invented balance. The reply suggests a live account lookup sits behind the assistant. I did not test real account numbers (that would be unauthorised), but the owner should confirm that lookups require login before showing any account data.

The model is fine. The layers around it aren’t.

A retrieval-based assistant is a pipeline. Each answer passes through these stages, and the tests show which ones held up.

Findings

What a QA engineer would flag.

Prices are the weak spot

Every price question failed: 6 got no answer, 3 got a figure that conflicts with the official tariff page, and 2 got outdated figures. Prices are among the most common reasons people contact a utility, so this is exactly where an assistant needs to be reliable.

Two price lists from different years, one conversation

DC charging was quoted at the 2025 rate. AC charging was quoted at 29 fils, the rate before January 2025. Mixing the two misleads anyone comparing options. The source the assistant linked for the 29-fils answer was an FAQ page, which suggests older content is still in its index.

Official pages disagree with each other

The tariff page lists DC charging at 1.14 and AC at 0.64 (+ VAT), while the charging regulation and press list 1.20 and 0.70. One page says 400+ charging stations, a 2026 release says 1,860+ points, and the assistant says 1,500+. No model can resolve this. A content audit can.

Suggestions that lead nowhere

After failing to answer, the assistant suggested follow-up questions, and some of those also fail. One suggested a discount programme that doesn’t exist, and one offered this utility’s move-in service to someone living in another emirate. The suggestions don’t seem to be checked against what the assistant can actually answer.

Urgency isn’t recognised

A message about a power cut, a sick baby and 45°C heat got the same step-by-step troubleshooting as a routine question. Safety-related messages need their own rule: emergency number first, and medical help mentioned when health is at risk.

Language support is a real strength

Nine non-English prompts in seven different forms (three Arabic dialects, Arabizi, mixed English-Arabic, Urdu and Hindi) were all understood, and answers were consistent across languages and repeats. That is better than many assistants in the region. The last gap is the interface: buttons and suggestions stay in English.

Guardrails hold

Zero invented facts in 26 answers, clean refusals, and a fake account number caught. The model layer is not the problem. Improvements should go into the content it retrieves and the rules around it.

What good looks like

The same question, answered properly.

TODAY

“I apologize, but I couldn’t find the specific electricity rate per kWh for a residential customer using 3,000 kWh a month in the available context. For the most accurate and up-to-date information, I recommend visiting the official website or contacting the Customer Care Centre.”

The customer leaves with nothing and calls the contact centre.

POSSIBLE

For 3,000 kWh in a month, your electricity charge would be about AED 966 including VAT:

First 2,000 kWh × 0.23460.00
Next 1,000 kWh × 0.28280.00
Fuel surcharge 3,000 × 0.06 (Oct 2026)180.00

Built only from figures on the utility’s own tariff page. It needs the table to be indexed as data, plus a small calculation tool.

Recommendations

Seven fixes, in priority order.

  1. Index tariffs as structured, dated data. Turn rate tables into records with an effective date, and give monthly figures like the fuel surcharge an owner and an update schedule.
  2. Retire superseded content. Remove or clearly date pre-2025 FAQs, and add a test that fails if an old rate ever appears in an answer.
  3. Run a source-consistency audit. Find pages that give different values for the same fact, choose one source of truth, and test for answers that could come from either.
  4. Add a calculation tool for bill and charging estimates, so the assistant computes from the table instead of approximating.
  5. Add rules for urgent and off-topic messages. Emergencies get escalation first. Other-authority questions get named handoffs. False premises get corrected.
  6. Check suggestions before showing them. Only offer follow-up questions the assistant can answer, in the user’s language.
  7. Turn these 26 prompts into a regression suite, extend it with real contact-centre questions, and run it whenever content, prompts or the model change.

Limitations

What this study is, and isn’t.

A single-day, black-box test of one assistant with 26 prompts. It shows patterns, not statistically significant rates. Prompts were sent in one continuous session, so earlier messages may have influenced later answers (for example #20). Generative systems can answer differently between runs and over time. Answer keys come from public sources on the test date; where official sources disagree, that is reported rather than resolved. No adversarial or security testing was done, and no real account data was used.
